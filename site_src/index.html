Feed STIX 2.1 Calendar Method

Influence operations, as the platforms and governments that took them down report them.

Each row is a network or operation that Meta, Google, OpenAI, TikTok or the EEAS has publicly disrupted or named. Apart from those, reported activity: independent reporters (DFRLab, CheckFirst, CORRECTIV) that have reported a tracked operation while it runs, active or quiet. The origin is the publisher's judgment of where the operators worked. It is never read as who directed them. Undertow keeps operations only: no account handles, links, domains or names of individuals.

Every row is the publisher's own report, and the counts show what publishers chose to disclose, not how much influence activity there is. How Undertow reads them.

Terms on this page
EEAS, FIMI
The European External Action Service, the EU's diplomatic service. It reports on foreign information manipulation and interference (FIMI), its term for these operations.
Origin, alignment, our confidence
Origin is where the publisher says the operators worked, never who directed them. Alignment is whose interests the publisher says an operation served, where it makes no attribution. Our confidence is about origin only: Low when one publisher states it, Moderate when two independent publishers state the same origin; never High.
DISARM, T0085.001
DISARM is an open catalogue of disinformation tactics and techniques. An id such as T0085.001 names one technique (here, "Develop AI-Generated Text"). Open a row to see each technique's name and the publisher's words that matched; the Method tab lists every rule.
Disclosure, takedown
A disclosure is one report, bulletin or announcement; it may list several networks the publisher took down or named.
Reported activity: active, quiet
An independent reporter wrote about a tracked operation. Active means a report in the last 180 days; quiet means none since, which is not evidence that it stopped.
DFRLab, CheckFirst, CORRECTIV
Independent researchers: the Atlantic Council's Digital Forensic Research Lab, the research company CheckFirst, and the German non-profit newsroom CORRECTIV.
G7 RRM
The G7 Rapid Response Mechanism, coordinated by Canada, which publishes statements on foreign threats to democracies. Its statements enter as hand-checked rows.
A2, B2, B3
Source grades. The letter is how reliable the source is (A completely, B usually), the figure how likely the report is true (2 probably, 3 possibly).
Q1 to Q4, H1, H2
Quarters and half-years of the calendar year (2026-H1 is January to June 2026).
STIX 2.1
A standard file format for sharing threat intelligence between tools; linked at the top.
RSS
A site's machine-readable list of its newest posts. Undertow reads reporters' RSS feeds, never their article pages.
In publishers' titles
SSA: sub-Saharan Africa. PRC: the People's Republic of China. CGTN: China Global Television Network, China's state broadcaster abroad. IUVM: International Union of Virtual Media, a network of sites that platforms have tied to Iran. RRN: Reliable Recent News, a site in the Doppelganger operation.

Disclosures over time

One mark per disclosure (a report, bulletin or announcement), sized by how many networks it lists. Google's bulletins are monthly within each quarter.

Origin by target

Networks counted by the origin the publisher states (rows) and the target countries it names (columns). Google and OpenAI name no targets, so they count under "none named". Regions such as "Sub-Saharan Africa" are in the table below.

Named operations

Operations a publisher names (Doppelganger, Storm-1516, Spamouflage...). Our confidence is about origin only and counts independent publishers that name the operation with the same origin: one is Low, two is Moderate. Origin is easy to fake, so nothing here is High.

Method and licences

Three questions, kept apart

  1. Did the takedown happen? The publisher reports its own action. Source grade A2: reliable on its own enforcement, not confirmable from outside.
  2. Where were the operators? The publisher's judgment, quoted as written, with the publisher's own level (high, moderate, none).
  3. Who directed it? Recorded only where a publisher names an operator. "Aligned with Russia", which the EEAS writes of operations it calls non-attributed, is shown as alignment, never as origin.

Our confidence follows the suite's rubric (tic-confidence-1.0): confidence mapping, method.

Event times in the feed: a disclosure carries the period its publisher gives, ended no later than the month of the report. An OpenAI item carries its post date, because OpenAI gives no activity period. A reporter's item runs from its first report to its last, or over the activity period the report states. A report date is not when an operation began, and a reporter going quiet is not evidence that it stopped.

What is never kept

Indicator values, account handles, URLs and domains, and the names of individuals. Meta's indicator tables are read only to count their rows. Every sentence kept from a publisher is scrubbed: links become "[link withheld]" and likely personal names "[name withheld]". The rule leans towards withholding.

DISARM techniques

DISARM Framework v (DISARM Foundation, CC BY-SA 4.0). A technique is attached only when a rule below matches the publisher's own words. It is Undertow's reading ("mapped"), not the publisher's claim.

Baseline: each publisher against its own record

Described, never rated. These are counts of what each publisher chose to disclose, not of influence activity, and no threshold is set, so nothing here is flagged as unusual. Each publisher is read at its own grain and is never added to another's.
